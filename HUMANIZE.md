# Humanizing outbound: what actually gives AI away

Written 2026-08-01 after auditing the twelve drafts in APPLICATIONS.md and
finding the real problem: zero buzzwords, but massive structural repetition.
That is the actual signal, and it is not what most "sound more human"
advice checks for.

## The diagnosis, measured

| pattern | count out of 12 |
| --- | --- |
| "155 commits" phrased near-identically | 10 |
| ends on "available now / available immediately" | 9 |
| opens with "Hi [Name]," | 7 |
| "no visa needed" repeated verbatim | 5 |
| corporate buzzwords (leverage, robust, seamless...) | 0 |

**A human writing twelve separate emails does not hit the same beats in the
same order every time.** A pipeline does. That shape is the tell, not the
word choice. If two recipients ever compare notes, or a founder just
pattern-matches subconsciously against the last "impressive but templated"
cold email they got, the structure gives it away before any sentence does.

## The real checklist

**1. Vary the shape, not just the words.**
Not every email needs both skill.supply and summon.company mentioned in
full, in the same order, with the same framing. Lead with whichever proof
point actually matches that posting's language. Mention the other briefly or
not at all. If every email is structured identically, it reads as a
template with the nouns swapped, however true and specific each fact is.

**2. Vary paragraph length and count on purpose.**
Real emails are uneven. Some are three short paragraphs. Some run longer
because the fit genuinely needed more explaining. Uniform paragraph length
across every message, the way a mail-merge produces, is itself a tell
independent of content.

**3. Do not end every message the same way.**
Ending on a question every single time is its own formula. Some should end
on a flat, confident statement. Some on a question. Mix it, and let the
specific email decide, not a template.

**4. Cut boilerplate lines that repeat verbatim.**
"No visa needed, available immediately" said the same way five times is a
tell even though every instance is true. Say the same fact differently each
time, or cut it where the context already implies it.

**5. Let sentence rhythm vary within one email.**
Real writing mixes short sentences with longer ones. A string of
similar-length, similarly-structured sentences in a row reads as generated
even when every fact in them is real and correct.

**6. One genuinely specific, non-reusable detail per email is worth more
than three generic proof points.**
The Logen draft referencing their own community page copy, and the
Holepunch draft naming Mathias's actual npm modules, are the strongest
moves in the whole set precisely because they cannot be copy-pasted into
any other email. Chase more of these, not fewer generic ones.

**7. Read it out loud before sending.**
The ear catches cadence that the eye reads past. If it sounds like a person
talking, including the small imperfections of speech, it is closer to done.
If it sounds like a list being narrated, it is not.

## What this is not

Not about injecting fake typos or fake casualness. Adam is a real engineer
with this exact profile; the fix is writing the way he would actually write
under time pressure, which is uneven, specific, and occasionally terse, not
performing a persona. Manufactured imperfection is its own tell.
