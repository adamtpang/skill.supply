# The Talent Trifecta

One business, three surfaces. **We quality the demand and quality the supply, then
matchmake and broker human talent capital.**

This file is the shared constitution for three repos. It lives in all of them and is
the same file everywhere. If you are a Claude Code session working in any one of
them, read this first so all three sessions speak the same language.

## The one-liners (the canon)

- 🎓 **company.university is the academy.** Where we raise a player's ceiling, on
  track for a named club. Every company gets a school.
- 🔬 **darktalent.tech is the scout and the stats engine.** How we measure and track
  players of all kinds, whether they came up through the academy or arrived from the
  transfer market.
- 🤝 **skill.supply is the transfer market.** Where deals get done between clubs and
  players, in tech and business.

## The business

Broker human talent capital. **Clubs (companies) pay on placement**, the headhunting
standard of 15 to 25% of first-year salary, 20 to 30% for senior and scarce tech
roles. **Players (candidates) never pay. No tuition, no ISA, ever.** Fees carry a
guarantee period, so a placement that does not stick gets clawed back. That is why
fit is not soft: fit protects the fee.

## The two levers to raise a talent pool

Exactly as in football:

1. **Transfer market**: import talent that is already good. Fast, expensive, no moat.
2. **Academy**: develop the talent you have. Slow, compounding, and the only one with
   a moat, because nobody can copy a pipeline.

Every great club runs both. We run both. What we do not take is low kinetic and low
potential, and we say so honestly rather than taking everyone.

## The numbers we scout on

- **Kinetic** = shipped output today (what darktalent scores now).
- **Potential** = learning velocity, the slope: output acceleration, new domains
  acquired, and the ETA to close a named gap.

**The trade:** buy high potential while the market only prices kinetic and
credentials. Pedigree is a discount, not a credit. A player at 38/100 today who
closes a real gap in 10 weeks is worth more than a static 60, and only we can see
that, because only we compute the ETA.

Overpriced by the market: elite school, big-tech logo, years of experience, title,
interview polish. Underpriced: shipped artifacts in use, **being depended on**,
learning velocity, having owned a number end to end, writing clarity.

## The loop

```
discover demand (real companies, real open roles)
   -> scout the player (darktalent: proof, pedigree gap, potential)
   -> gap analysis + ETA (skill.supply: how many weeks to be the obvious hire)
   -> academy (company.university: close the gaps, emit verified artifacts)
   -> the artifact writes back to the card (proof compounds)
   -> broker the deal (skill.supply: warm intro, placement, fee)
```

Every attempt manufactures new proof. That is the flywheel nobody can copy, because
auto-appliers produce noise and we produce signal that compounds to the player.

## Standing rules

- **Demand-first, always.** Secure the club before you train the player. Supply-first
  training is why bootcamps fail to place people.
- **The student never pays.** The company pays on success.
- **Never sell to NS members.** They are supply and they only receive value. Revenue
  comes from clubs and business counterparties.
- **Proof over pedigree**, in every ranking and every score.
- **No em dashes** anywhere: code, copy, docs, or model output. Use commas, colons,
  periods, parentheses.
- **Honest ETAs.** A flattering estimate is a lie that costs someone months.
- Nothing about a person or a company is claimed unless it is defensible from the
  input or clearly labeled an estimate.

- **Ownership boundaries:**
  - `darktalent.tech` owns scoring, signals, and the card. It never brokers.
  - `company.university` owns curricula and verified artifacts. It never scores.
  - `skill.supply` owns discovery, matching, the ETA, the intro, and the fee.
- **The shared object is the card.** Its scores come from darktalent, its artifacts
  from company.university, and it is transacted by skill.supply.

## Reference model: the clubs that actually print money

The profitable clubs do not buy stars. They **scout undervalued, develop, and sell**,
then repeat. The numbers are not small:

| Club | Academy transfer revenue | The edge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Benfica | about €516M in a decade | Scouting network in Argentina and Brazil, buying unknowns for almost nothing |
| Ajax | about €376M | Academy plus genuinely playing the graduates |
| Lyon | about €370M | Long-horizon academy investment |
| Brighton | Caicedo under £5M, sold at a British record | Data-driven scouting in ponds nobody else fishes (Ecuador, Japan, Belgium) |

Three transferable lessons:

1. **The cycle is the business.** Acquire cheap, develop, realize value, reinvest in
   scouting. One placement is a transaction; the cycle is a company.
2. **Fish where others do not look.** Brighton's edge is the pond, not the budget.
   Ours is dark talent: the builders LinkedIn cannot see.
3. **The academy is a long-horizon asset.** Benfica's took years to compound. Expect
   the same and do not judge company.university on its first cohort.

Apply all three to human talent capital in tech.
