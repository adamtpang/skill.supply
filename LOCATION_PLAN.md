## How to read the numbers

Three source classes are used and they are NOT interchangeable.

1. **Official.** Government immigration portals, US federal health agencies, national statistics. Treated as authoritative.
2. **Measured.** Ookla Speedtest Global Index (real device measurements), airline and OTA fare pages (real inventory at a point in time).
3. **Crowd-sourced.** Numbeo. Numbeo is user-submitted, unaudited, and thin in small markets. Every Numbeo figure below carries its stated last-update date and, where shown, its contributor count. Guam's Numbeo page had **65 entries by 10 contributors in 12 months**, which is far too thin to trust the way a Mexico City page (1,395 entries, 142 contributors) can be trusted.

Where sources disagree, both numbers are printed. Nothing is averaged.
Where a figure could not be verified, it says **not verified** and names what would verify it.

### Visa reality

**Perfect and permanent.** Guam is an unincorporated US territory. A US citizen needs no visa, no entry permission, no exit, and no clock. He can work locally on day one with no employment authorization step. There is no version of Guam where he has to leave. **This is the single strongest legal position on the entire list and nothing else comes close.**

Travel note: Guam-to-mainland routings are domestic via Honolulu, but the cheapest published fares route through Asian hubs (Incheon, Narita, Manila), which are international sectors.

### Health coverage: the Guam trap

Healthcare.gov states that **if you live in Guam you are not eligible to use the Marketplace to enroll in health coverage** unless you also qualify as a resident of one of the 50 states or DC. The US territories were **explicitly excluded from the ACA Medicaid expansion mechanism**, leaving a coverage gap for low-income adults who do not meet Guam's more restrictive pre-expansion income thresholds (MACPAC, "Medicaid and CHIP in the Territories").

So a jobless 24-year-old on Guam has **no ACA subsidy path and likely no Medicaid path**. Cost of a private individual plan on Guam: **not verified**. To verify: contact StayWell Insurance, NetCare, or TakeCare (Guam's private carriers) directly for an individual under-26 quote, and check whether he can remain on a parent's plan under the ACA dependent-coverage-to-26 rule, which applies to plans regardless of where he lives.

**Action item that could dominate this whole analysis:** if a parent has employer coverage, ACA dependent coverage to age 26 may still cover him. That is worth one phone call and it is worth more than any cost-of-living table on this page.

### Visa reality (this is the part most guides get wrong)

**Visa-exempt entry: 90 days.** A US citizen receives a **Short Term Visit Pass (social)** on arrival, valid up to 90 days including the arrival day. The **Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC)** must be completed before travel. Passport validity 6 months.

**Extension: effectively not available.** In most cases this pass **cannot be extended**. Extensions are issued only in extreme circumstances such as illness, accident, or an event endangering life. Do not plan around getting one.

**Work status:** it is a visit pass, not a work pass. Remote work for a foreign employer on it is **not authorized**. Overstay or wrong-visa penalties include entry ban, detention, or fines.

**What happens at the 90-day limit:** he has to leave. Land border runs to Singapore and re-entry are common informal practice but **not a documented entitlement**, and grant length is at the officer's discretion. **Not verified as reliable.** To verify: ask Malaysian Immigration (imi.gov.my) directly, or accept that this is discretionary and plan a real exit.

**The legal path: DE Rantau Nomad Pass.**
- 12 months, renewable once, 24 months total
- Application fee about **MYR 1,000 (about USD 230)**
- Income bar: **tech applicants above USD 24,000/year (about USD 2,000/month), non-tech above USD 60,000/year**
- Requires proof of remote work: active contracts with foreign-based companies or clients, proof of income
- A statutory exemption on foreign-sourced income received in Malaysia by resident individuals has applied **through 2026-12-31**, meaning many DE Rantau holders faced an effective 0% Malaysian tax on foreign earnings during the exemption window. US citizens still owe US filing obligations regardless.

**The catch that decides everything:** DE Rantau requires proof of existing remote income. He is job-hunting. **Whether his current contract/bridge income clears the USD 24,000/year tech bar is not verified.** To verify: total the last 3-12 months of contract invoices and compare against USD 2,000/month; then check the MDEC DE Rantau portal's exact documentary requirements.

### Vietnam visa reality (applies to both Vietnamese cities)

- **No visa-free arrangement exists between the US and Vietnam.** A visa is required.
- **E-visa: up to 90 days, single or multiple entry.** Official fee from the Vietnam Immigration Department portal: **USD 25 single entry, USD 50 multiple entry**. Processing 3 working days. Entirely online.
- **The e-visa cannot be extended or renewed from inside Vietnam.** He must exit and apply for a new one from abroad. This is confirmed on the official portal and by multiple guides.
- **Vietnam has no digital nomad visa and no remote-work visa.** The 90-day e-visa is a tourist visa and the underlying law barring employment is unchanged. Remote work on it is **tolerated but not authorized**. This is a gray zone, not a permission.
- Tax residency at **183 days or more** in a calendar year.

**Verdict: 90 days, then a hard exit. Same practical ceiling as Malaysia, with a cheaper and more predictable reset (leave, re-apply online, come back) but no legal work basis at all.**

## A note on unreliable "digital nomad" guidance

One widely-shared 2026 benchmark post claims **38 ms VPN latency from Chiang Mai to US-East** and 44 ms from Bangkok. **This is physically impossible.** The great-circle distance from Bangkok to New York is roughly 13,900 km. Light in optical fibre travels at roughly 200,000 km/s, so even a perfectly straight fibre path gives a round-trip floor of about **140 ms**, and real routed paths are longer. The same post also asserts a "Kuala Lumpur Digital Nomad Visa (max income USD 80k)", which does not match MDEC's actual DE Rantau thresholds of USD 24,000 tech and USD 60,000 non-tech.

Treat every nomad-blog city ranking as untrustworthy unless each number traces to an official portal or a measurement platform. **Actual measured latency from any of these cities to US cloud regions: not verified.** To verify: run `ping` and `mtr` against a us-west-2 and a us-east-1 endpoint from the actual apartment or coworking space, before signing anything.

### Scenario C: nothing lands for 3 months

**Answer: Guam.**

Reasoning: at the end of three empty months the binding constraint stops being cost per month and becomes **the ability to stop burning entirely**. Guam is the only option that delivers all four of the things that matter when the money runs out:

1. **No visa clock, ever.** Every SEA option forces an exit and a paid re-entry exactly when he can least afford one. Malaysia's 90 days would already be spent.
2. **Rent can go to near zero.** Family on-island collapses the biggest line item, taking burn from a solo **USD 2,950/mo** to **USD 850-1,450/mo** or less. Nothing else on this list can go to zero.
3. **He can legally take any job on day one.** Guam's unemployment was **3.4% in March 2026** with private nonsupervisory average hourly earnings of **USD 19.10**, and the family wholesale business (International Distributors) is a real employer, not a hypothetical one.
4. **A US address, US bank, and US phone number** keep the application pipeline alive and remove "must reside in the US" as a disqualifier.

The costs he is accepting: **no local AI job market or meetup scene whatsoever** (not verified that any exists, and no evidence of one was found), **expensive flights out at USD 1,097-1,377**, and **no ACA Marketplace eligibility**, because territory residents cannot enroll and the territories were excluded from Medicaid expansion.

**Before committing to Guam, make one phone call:** if a parent has employer health coverage, ACA dependent coverage to age 26 may still cover him at 24. That single call is worth more than every cost table above.

**If Guam is ruled out by that health question or by family circumstances, the fallback for Scenario C is Manila**, on the strength of the **36-month legal ceiling with no income test**, at **USD 1,070/mo** and with nonstop Philippine Airlines service to San Francisco.

## The single decision deadline

**Last sensible date to decide: Friday 2026-09-04.**

Three clocks converge on that date.

1. **The move-out clock.** He is already moving out "within weeks". Any decision inside 14 days of move-out forces either a bridge hotel or a rushed lease, both of which cost more than the decision saves.
2. **The airfare clock.** The OTAs' own guidance for these routes is to book roughly **40 days ahead** for Guam-SFO and **2 months ahead** for international long-haul, with fares rising sharply inside the 14-to-21-day window. Deciding on 2026-09-04 keeps a mid-to-late-September departure inside a 21-day-plus booking window on every candidate route.
3. **The Malaysian 90-day clock.** His visit pass expires 90 days from his last entry stamp and **cannot realistically be extended**. Overstaying risks an entry ban, detention, or fines.

**Two inputs are missing and both should be nailed down today, not on 2026-09-04:**

- **His exact move-out date.** The deadline is really "14 days before move-out, and no later than 2026-09-04", whichever comes first.
- **The date on his last Malaysian entry stamp.** Count 90 days forward. If that date falls before 2026-09-04, **the visa clock is the deadline and it overrides everything above.**
