What they actually do (sourced)

LangChain builds the tooling layer for AI agents: open-source frameworks plus a commercial platform for observing, evaluating, deploying, and operating agents in production.

Self-described, from the live Ashby job description fetched 2026-08-22: "our mission is to make intelligent agents ubiquitous. We build the foundation for agent engineering in the real world, helping developers move from prototypes to production-ready AI agents that teams can rely on."

The product surface, as stated in the current postings:

  • Open source frameworks: LangChain, LangGraph, and Deep Agents.
  • LangSmith, the commercial platform, covering Observability, Evaluation, Deployment, Fleet, and Sandboxes.
  • LangSmith Engine, described in the postings as "newly launched" for autonomous agent improvement. There is also a live "Research Engineer, LangSmith Engine" posting (NYC and SF, posted 2026-08-14), which corroborates that this is an active new product line, not marketing language.
  • SmithDB, a database product with its own engineering manager, senior engineer, and infrastructure engineer roles currently open. This is a real internal database effort, visible only through the job board.

Traction claims, all from LangChain's own current job descriptions (self-reported, but specific and repeated verbatim across postings):

  • "100M+ monthly open source downloads"
  • "6,000+ active LangSmith customers"
  • "5 of the Fortune 10 use LangSmith in production (+ 35% of the Fortune 500 overall)"
  • Named customers: Klarna, Clay, Coinbase, Workday, Lyft, Cloudflare, Harvey, Rippling, Vanta, LinkedIn, Monday.com, Nvidia, and Bridgewater.

Cross-check against the Series B announcement (langchain.com/blog/series-b, 2025-10-20), which stated "combined 90M monthly downloads" for langchain and langgraph, "35 percent of the Fortune 500 use our services", and that LangSmith monthly trace volume had "12x'd year over year". Interview process, third-party only

LangChain does not publish a process on its careers page or in any posting. Third-party accounts (Exponent interview guides and candidate write-ups, 2025-2026) describe the Deployed Engineer process as: a recruiter or hiring-manager screen of about 30 minutes; a take-home to build a presentation about a LangChain feature; a second assignment to build an AI agent against specified criteria and customer needs; and a peer interview described as conversational rather than gotcha-style, covering agents, coding assistants, observability, evaluation, and customer adoption. Treat all of that as unverified third-party reporting. But if it is even approximately right, the process is take-home-heavy and artifact-driven, which is the best possible shape for a candidate whose case is portfolio-based rather than resume-based.

What they actually do (3-5 sentences, sourced)

Emerald AI writes software that makes AI datacenters' electricity draw flexible, so a datacenter can be treated as a dispatchable grid asset instead of a fixed block of load. Their product, Emerald Conductor, is described on their own site as "the intelligent interface between the world's two most valuable infrastructure networks: power grids and data centers," and it orchestrates training, fine-tuning, and inference workloads across a network of datacenters in real time (emeraldai.co, read 2026-08-22). They claim three flexibility levers: temporal (pausing batchable workloads during grid stress), spatial (shifting workloads to regions with available power), and resource (dispatching batteries alongside compute), which they say can unlock "100 GW of capacity for AI data centers" (self-reported headline claim). The core technical proof is a Phoenix field demonstration with Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, NVIDIA, EPRI, and Salt River Project on 2025-05-01 and 2025-05-03, where a 256-GPU cluster cut power 25% below average base load and held it for 3 hours during peak demand, ramping down and up over 15 minutes, without unacceptable workload degradation. That result was peer reviewed and published in Nature Energy, and a preprint is public at arXiv 2507.00909, which is unusually strong evidence for a company this young.

Critical, under-reported update: as of August 2026 Emerald AI is no longer only a grid software company. Two roles published 2026-08-05 and 2026-08-06 state they are "building the world's first power flexible managed cloud infrastructure" and hiring to "architect and stand up our managed cloud services from end to end," with a companion PM role to "define and launch Emerald Cloud Services," described as "a true 0-1 product role" targeting "enterprises, AI labs, and infrastructure providers" (Emerald AI Ashby postings, read 2026-08-22).
