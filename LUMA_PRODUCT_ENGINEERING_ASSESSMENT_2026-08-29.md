# Luma Product Engineering assessment

Checked 2026-08-29 against the live role, Luma's current careers page, and
current employee evidence.

## Verdict

Reject [Product Engineer](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/lumaai/93802d6a-cdad-49e1-8154-9cf005d7e3b8)
from the S-tier campaign despite exceptional role fit.

The role is unusually friendly to a high-output engineer early in their career.
It accepts two years of experience or exceptional proof through a real-user
project, open source, or ownership of a nontrivial system. The $168,000 to
$252,000 band, equity, $1.3 billion or more in reported funding, 30 million or
more users, and Redwood City location clear the other gates.

People does not. Current employee evidence is small and company-wide rather than
exact-team evidence, but it includes a July 2026 report of extreme
micromanagement, leadership overriding reason and data, recurring overnight and
weekend work across functions, and chaos without planning. An August 2026 review
separately reports strong people and products alongside missing structure. With
10 reviews, a 3.7 overall rating, 71 percent recommendation, and 3.2 work-life
rating, the evidence is too negative and too current to certify an S-tier people
environment. The unnamed direct manager makes the uncertainty worse.

**Target quality: 50. Status: `rejected`. Candidate match: 97.**

## Candidate fit

Adam's approximately 28 to 29 supportable paid engineering months clear the
two-year path. His deployed TypeScript agent product, tested browser harness,
known-ground-truth evaluation, and open-source work match Luma's explicit
alternative-proof language. The main technical gaps are production work in
Python, Go, or Java at Luma's scale and verified ownership of a system used by a
large user base.

Those gaps do not cause the rejection. The people evidence does.

## Decision rule

Do not spend an application on this role under the S-tier-only campaign. Reopen
only if a named direct manager and multiple current exact-team engineers supply
specific, consistent evidence that normal hours are sustainable, crunch is
exceptional and followed by recovery, decisions follow evidence, and engineers
receive coaching without micromanagement.
